# References

Reliability: ★★★ peer-reviewed / primary scholarly · ★★ practitioner or academic teaching material

## Music system and tuning

- ★★★ Andrew Tracey, **"The System of the Mbira"**. Paper from the 7th Symposium on Ethnomusicology (1988,
  publ. ILAM 1989), reprinted in *Journal of the International Library of African Music*.
  The source for the dyad system, the 48-pulse sequence, `334 343 434 333`, C/E/F/A standards,
  and the 200–300 cent equi-spacing tendency.
  <https://journal.ru.ac.za/index.php/africanmusic/article/download/1229/656/658>
- ★★★ Paul Berliner, ***The Soul of Mbira*** (1978/1993), Univ. of California Press. Key layout
  (B/L/R manuals), tuning variability (pp. 54–72), Nyamaropa transcriptions (p. 76), kushaura/kutsinhira.
  *Not read directly for this research. Cited via secondary sources.*
- ★★★ Andrew Tracey, *How to Play the Mbira (dza vadzimu)* (ILAM, 1970). Notation and zvara.
- ★★ B. Michael Williams, **"Getting Started with Mbira dzaVadzimu"**, *Percussive Notes*, Aug 1997.
  Four 12-pulse sections, kushaura/kutsinhira offset, Nyamaropa tablature.
  <https://bmichaelwilliams.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/02/PNGettingStartedMbira.pdf>
- ★★★ Martin Scherzinger, "Temporal Geometries of an African Music", *Music Theory Online* 16.4 (2010).
  <https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.10.16.4/mto.10.16.4.scherzinger.pdf>
- ★★ Scherzinger interview on the buzz and inherent patterns (Afropop Worldwide).
  <https://afropop.org/articles/phantom-patterns-martin-scherzinger-on-shona-mbira-music>
- ★★ Tinotenda: named tunings. <https://www.tinotenda.org/tunings.htm>.
  Kushaura and kutsinhira. <https://tinotenda.org/wp/64-2/>
- ★★ sympathetic-resonances.org: notation systems and layouts for nyunga nyunga and dzavadzimu.
  <https://sympathetic-resonances.org/help/mbira>, <https://sympathetic-resonances.org/help/nyunga_nyunga>
- ★ Wikipedia, "Mbira". Key counts per instrument type, tuning names, machachara.
  <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbira>
- ★ Kalimba Magic: nyunga nyunga cents chart (retailer, measurement origin unknown).
  <https://www.kalimbamagic.com/blog/item/african-karimba-resources>

## Acoustics

- ★★★ **"Vibrational frequencies and tuning of the African mbira"**. Measured spectrum: dominant
  overtones ≈ 5× and 14× the fundamental. FEM shows transverse modes dominate radiation. Fundamentals
  ≈ 117–948 Hz. (Author/venue not confirmed in this research. Abstract via CORE/ResearchGate.)
  <https://core.ac.uk/works/83488459> ·
  <https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5604142_Vibrational_frequencies_and_tuning_of_the_African_mbira>
- ★★★ D. M. F. Chapman, **"Characterizing the sound of an African thumb piano (kalimba)"**, JASA (2008)
  / ASA 155th meeting 5aMUf1. Clamped–supported–free beam, f₁/f₀ ≈ 5.3–5.9, fast-decaying
  overtones. Popular summary: <https://acoustics.org/pressroom/httpdocs/155th/chapman.htm>
- ★★★ D. M. F. Chapman, "Investigating the sound of an African thumb piano (kalimba)",
  *Canadian Acoustics* 34(3) 2006. The overtone structure is sensitive to mounting geometry.
  <https://jcaa.caa-aca.ca/index.php/jcaa/article/view/1805>
- ★★ "The tuning of Mbira reeds: a contribution to the craft of Mbira making", *African Music* (JILAM).
  <https://journal.ru.ac.za/index.php/africanmusic/article/view/1440>
- ★ UBC PHYS341 student wiki, kalimba resonator box frequencies.
  <https://wiki.ubc.ca/Course:PHYS341/KalimbaResonator>

  MIT Press, 1991.
